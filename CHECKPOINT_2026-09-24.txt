1. HEADLINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The multi-camera system is integrated in the container like the nose camera.
The top pair (aux_left, aux_right) is received as ROS topics at 30 fps, stamped on
the same ROS clock as IMU and DVL, and recorded by record.launch.py, which now has one
switch per sensor and refuses to record unless every selected stream is publishing.

Connecting to the camera system took several days; the causes are in section 3.

3. WHAT WAS WRONG, IN ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  Physical connection (cause of "the camera system is not reachable").
     From 2026-09-17 to 2026-09-22 the camera computer sent not a single packet on
     the tether: ARP scans of 10.42.0.x, 192.168.1.x and 192.168.2.x, an IPv6
     all-hosts ping and a 5-minute capture during a camera power cycle found only
     BlueOS and the DVL. The colleague's laptop showed the same (10.42.0.5 FAILED,
     10.42.0.1 INCOMPLETE in its neighbour table). On 2026-09-22 the tether adapter
     had also negotiated only 10 Mbit/s half duplex. The cables were fixed on
     2026-09-24; after that the link is 100 Mbit/s full duplex and the camera
     computer answers at 10.42.0.5 and 192.168.1.4 with ttl=64.

3.2  False positives over Wi-Fi.
     The university network also uses 10.42.x.x. Without a 10.42.0.x address on the
     tether adapter, pings to 10.42.0.1 / 10.42.0.5 went out over Wi-Fi and got
     answers from unrelated machines (ttl=55/56, six routers away). Only ttl=64 means
     a device on the tether.

3.3  The colleague's "working ping" was his own laptop.
     10.42.0.156 (and 192.168.1.1, 192.168.2.1, 192.168.17.156) are addresses of his
     own Ethernet port, so pinging 10.42.0.156 always succeeded.

3.4  Changing addresses.
     10.42.0.x is Ubuntu's "Shared to other computers" range: the sharing machine is
     10.42.0.1 and hands out addresses, which is why the camera computer was seen as
     .1, .5 or .156 at different times. It currently keeps 10.42.0.5.

3.5  Topside addresses.
     The tether adapter needs 192.168.2.1/24, 192.168.1.1/24 and 10.42.0.1/24, no
     gateway. A gateway on the tether steals the default route from Wi-Fi; a /8 mask
     on 10.42.0.x swallows the university network. See TOPSIDE_SETUP.md 2b.

3.6  Services do not answer from Fast DDS.
     With the addresses right, camera_status requests reach the camera system and its
     topics reach us, but no service reply ever arrives: it runs CycloneDDS and our
     container Fast DDS. Topics interoperate, services do not. Not needed for
     receiving and recording the streams, which were already running.

8. SESSION PIPELINE  (added later the same day)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  Why the /ba services did not answer
     From our Fast DDS container the camera_status request reached the camera
     system but no reply ever came back; its topics did arrive. The camera system
     runs CycloneDDS. With ros-humble-rmw-cyclonedds-cpp in the image and the call
     made on CycloneDDS bound to 192.168.1.1, every service answers.
     (An earlier theory - replies routed to the Wi-Fi address via the missing
     gateway 10.42.0.1 - was real but not sufficient: the laptop now owns 10.42.0.1
     and Fast DDS calls still got no reply.)

8.2  multicam command (scripts/multicam.py)
     status / start / stop / lights / shutdown. Tested on the vehicle:
       start aux_left aux_right   -> ~270 packets/s per camera, check 30.0 Hz each
       stop                       -> 0 packets
       lights 50                  -> lights on (seen); needed configure + activate,
                                     the controller boots 'unconfigured' and then
                                     answers done=True with the lights dark
       stop all / shutdown        -> only cameras that are streaming (stopping an
                                     idle camera is refused and looked like FAILED)
     After a reboot of the camera computer: software up in ~2 min, nothing streams
     until `multicam start` (watched for 4 min).

8.3  QGroundControl on the same laptop (PC-only changes)
     IMU driver moved to UDP 14551 (BlueOS "Multi-camera" endpoint = identical copy:
     1107 vs 1111 packets in 5 s). Nose video: a relay in bluerov2.launch.py binds
     192.168.2.1:5600 and copies each packet to 127.0.0.1:5600 (QGC) and :5602
     (driver). Stand-in test: QGC-like listeners got 823 MAVLink packets / 3 s and
     4103 video packets / 4 s while the drivers ran (IMU 101.8 Hz, nose 32.4 fps).
     Confirmed with the real QGroundControl: vehicle connected, video shown.

8.4  session_start.launch.py
     preflight.py (Pi ping, ArduSub heartbeat on 14551, DVL ping + TCP, camera
     computer ping + camera_status) -> drivers -> multicam start -> lights 50 % 5 s
     -> stream check -> SESSION READY. Two cold starts:
       1st: DVL on DHCP, came up at 192.168.2.128 only after 441 s -> READY ~7.5 min
       2nd: DVL set static (below): DVL 0 s, Pi+IMU 69 s, camera computer 128 s,
            SESSION READY 146 s. Stream check IMU 101.2, DVL 4.3, nose 32.3,
            aux_left 30.3, aux_right 30.3 Hz.

8.5  DVL fixed address
     The DVL was on DHCP (saved /api/v1/ip: dhcp=true) and fell back to its factory
     192.168.194.95 until BlueOS gave it .128. Set static in its web page
     (Configuration -> Static, 192.168.2.128/24, gw/dns 192.168.2.2), power cycled:
     now dhcp=false, .128 answers before BlueOS is up; 192.168.194.95 stays as backup.

8.6  Camera computer network (read over SSH)
     Already fixed: profile nm_conn_static (only autoconnect) = 192.168.1.4/24 +
     10.42.0.5/24, gw 10.42.0.1. Other profiles off (custom_static with
     192.168.2.5/24 + 10.42.0.5/8, two DHCP) - probably why addresses "changed" before.

8.7  session_stop.launch.py
     multicam shutdown: stop streaming cameras -> lights 0 on channels 1-3 ->
     sshpass + ssh nvidia@10.42.0.5 "sudo -S poweroff" -> wait until it stops
     answering + 10 s. Tested: 57 s, camera computer off (100 % loss), BlueOS on.
     Login in data/.multicam_ssh (git-ignored, chmod 600), template in
     bluerov2_bringup/config/multicam_ssh.example. The password is the one in the
     lab's multi-camera user guide; it is NOT in the repository.

8.8  Disk: 3.4 GB free after three image rebuilds - run `docker image prune`.
